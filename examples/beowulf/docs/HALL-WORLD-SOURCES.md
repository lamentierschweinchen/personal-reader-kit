# The world of the hall — evidence and image record

The companion was accepted for the owner-authorized public Beowulf edition after parent and independent editorial review. Its release rights and audience are recorded in SOURCE-RIGHTS.md. The imagined room is an editorial navigation device. It is not an archaeological reconstruction or an assertion that the finds were present in a single hall.

## Historical sources

- Tom Christensen, “Lejre beyond the legend – the archaeological evidence,” *Settlement and Coastal Research in the Southern North Sea Region* 33 (2010), 237–254: [museum-hosted paper](https://romu.dk/wp-content/uploads/sites/21/2018/07/Lejre-beyond-the-legend.pdf). Fredshøj discussion, figures 17–20 and pages 249–252: relative placement of the smaller house, large building and stone heap; the paper's treatment of the heap is interpretive. The companion's schematic shows the three pairs of roof-bearing postholes in the smaller building and the reported distances between pairs; crosswise spacing and circle sizes are schematic. It is not a surveyed posthole plan or a copy of the figure. The same paper distinguishes the later Lejre legends from evidence in the poem.
- English Heritage, [Description of Birdoswald Roman Fort](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/birdoswald-roman-fort-hadrians-wall/history-and-stories/history/description/) and [Uncovering the Secrets of Birdoswald](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/birdoswald-roman-fort-hadrians-wall/history-and-stories/the-secrets-of-birdoswald/). The visible timber posts are modern markers, while the exposed granary remains are Roman. The post-Roman building, hearths and proposed use have a different history from the Danish site.
- British Museum, [Sutton Hoo and Europe, Room 41 guide](https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2021-05/large_print_guide_room_41.pdf), especially pp. 5–8, 16, 39–40 and 53–54: helmet, silver platter, horn mounts and clasps. [Museum guide to the original and replica helmets](https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2021-02/young_friends_sleepover_sutton_hoo_helmet.pdf). [Museum object view of the horns](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/drinking-horns-from-the-ship-burial-at-sutton-hoo/twFuvoV610_wMQ) and [platter](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/the-anastasius-platter-from-the-ship-burial-at-sutton-hoo/3QGqd1-ZQK6MfA). The museum's [members' trail](https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2021-06/sutton_hoo_members_supporters_trail.pdf) explains the platter's control stamps and identifies gift exchange as likely, not a documented itinerary.
- British Museum, [Silk Roads activity cards, KS3](https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2024-09/Silk_Roads_Exhibition_activity_cards_KS3.pdf), Northwest Europe card: the Sutton Hoo shoulder clasp and its garnet sources. Sources of material are not an established route, seller or place of manufacture.
- The cup sequence refers to Hall's translation, fit X, lines 55–73; the companion paraphrases the scene and does not assert that the Sutton Hoo vessels were used by those characters.

The small Fredshøj diagram summarizes only three pairs of posts described for the smaller house in Christensen, p. 249 and figure 18. The 6.3 m and 3.8 m intervals are the stated distances between pairs; the spacing across the house and drawn circle sizes are schematic. The interactive lines show the excavator's inference of paired roof supports, not surviving beams. It is an original diagram, not a facsimile, and the excavator's figure remains linked for the actual archaeological plan. No geometric measurements were taken from the protected graphic.

- `site/hall-whorl.jpg`: Tom Brindle / Northamptonshire County Council, credited to [The Portable Antiquities Scheme and Trustees of the British Museum](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Anglo-Saxon_stone_spindle_whorl,_obverse_(FindID_79324).jpg), [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Original 468 × 459 pixel Commons image, unchanged. The record identifies the sandstone whorl and its decoration, diameter and find county, and dates it broadly AD 410–1066; it does not establish a precise date or place it in a woman's grave.
- Ashmolean Museum, [Loomweight from Sutton Courtenay](https://anglosaxondiscovery.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/Life/clothes/loomweight_sc.html): 15 loom weights from House 20, many grouped on one side, with a spindle whorl and other finds. These objects are separate from the Northamptonshire whorl and the Sutton Hoo clasp. The museum's reconstruction of a loom's location is a qualified interpretation.
- Institute of Conservation, [Nigel Williams and Sutton Hoo](https://www.icon.org.uk/resource/digging-deeper-nigel-williams-and-sutton-hoo.html): the 1940s assembly was later dismantled and Williams studied the fragments and joins for the later reconstruction. The Hall fit VI–VII threshold account and fit V boar signs are paraphrased from the unchanged source edition.
