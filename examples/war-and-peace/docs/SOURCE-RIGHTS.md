# War and Peace: source and reuse record

Reviewed for this public example on 21 October 2026. This is the complete Louise
and Aylmer Maude translation with twelve images from the 1893 illustration album.
The release is free to read. The novel, historical images and fonts are separate
from the MIT-licensed reader software. No library endorsement or Creative Commons
licence is claimed.

## The 1893 album

The [Presidential Library record](https://www.prlib.ru/item/1988888) identifies an
album published by Evgeny Evdokimov in St Petersburg, with an 1893 censorship
date, as a supplement to *Sever*. It describes eight text pages and twelve printed
illustration/portrait leaves. The printed album copy is held by the State Public
Historical Library, Moscow; its digital reproduction is supplied by the
Presidential Library. This credits the printed copy and scan provider, without
claiming that either holds every original watercolour.

The album reproduces a portrait by Ilya Repin and eleven scenes by Nikolay
Karazin, Aleksey Kivshenko and Leonid Pasternak. The twelve WebP files are the
unchanged reviewed display derivatives: four public-viewer JPEG regions per
plate were assembled, cropped to the printed image and rotated where needed.
There was no colour correction, reconstruction or new illustration. The exact
public source URLs, leaf numbers, crop/rotation values, dimensions and hashes are
in [EDITION-INVENTORY.json](EDITION-INVENTORY.json); final file pins are in
[edition-lock.json](../edition-lock.json).

The reuse basis is the public-domain artwork and the absence of new original
authorship in these faithful mechanical reproductions:

- In the United States, the 1893 published contributions are beyond the maximum
  95-year published-work term. Foreign copyright restoration does not extend
  that term indefinitely: it supplies the remainder of the applicable US term.
  See the Copyright Office's [duration guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ15a.pdf)
  and [restoration guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ38b.pdf).
- The Copyright Office's [Compendium, section 909.3(A)](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap900/ch900-visual-art.pdf)
  distinguishes original visual authorship from a mechanical or exact reproduction
  of an existing work. Effort, investment or a change of medium alone does not
  supply new copyright in the reproduced image.
- The UK Intellectual Property Office's [digital-images notice](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-notice-digital-images-photographs-and-the-internet/copyright-notice-digital-images-photographs-and-the-internet)
  explains why a faithful digitisation of an older artwork is unlikely to contain
  the creative choices needed for new copyright. That guidance acknowledges the
  legal debate; this record does not recast it as an unconditional worldwide rule.
  The underlying artists are also outside ordinary [UK artistic-work terms](https://www.gov.uk/copyright/how-long-copyright-lasts).

Institutional biographical records support the artists' dates: Repin, 1844–1930
([National Gallery](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/ilya-repin-study-of-an-old-man));
Kivshenko, 1851–1895
([Getty ULAN](https://www.getty.edu/vow/ULANFullDisplay?find=&nation=&role=&subjectid=500026596));
Karazin, 1842–1908 in the library catalogue (1909 under the later calendar,
which does not affect the term conclusion)
([Presidential Library](https://www.prlib.ru/item/441232)); and Pasternak,
1862–1945 ([Ashmolean](https://www.ashmolean.org/article/autumnal-scenes)).

## The digital service's terms

The item record was available through the anonymous public catalogue/viewer. It
does not state an item-specific prohibition on reproduction or offer a Creative
Commons grant. The site's general copyright footer is not itself evidence of
original copyright in the 1893 art or a mechanical scan of it.

The currently linked [library documents](https://www.prlib.ru/about_docs) include
[reading-room rules dated 25 December 2024](https://www.prlib.ru/sites/default/files/u535/pravila_polzovaniya_chz_fgbu_pb.pdf).
Their stated scope is registered use of the library's reading rooms; the rules
describe admission, reader registration, workstations, copying services and
restrictions on reading-room users. The present assessment did not find a
portal-wide reuse agreement presented to, or accepted by, the anonymous public
viewer, nor an item-specific restriction attaching to these public scan URLs.
It does not treat reading-room copying permission as a public reuse licence.

This release therefore relies on the old artwork and faithful-reproduction
basis above, not on an invented open licence, permission inferred from the
absence of a badge, or a licence to copy the library's database as a whole.
Only this identified historical album's twelve images are included. Source
credit and links remain with the pictures. No new outreach, payment or agreement
was used. A later reuse in another jurisdiction or from different source files
requires its own assessment.

## Picture apparatus and exclusions

The reviewed captions, scene anchors, artist credits, explicit chapter gates and
historical reference links are retained. The portrait is available at the title
page; the eleven story scenes require the reader's explicit chapter permission.
Opening a picture's source passage does not advance the main reading place.
Pasternak's corresponding first-ball watercolour is catalogued by the Tolstoy
Museum, but identity with the exact sheet reproduced here remains unproved.
The other original watercolours' present locations are not asserted.

The unrelated decorative cover supplied with the Gutenberg HTML has uncertain
provenance. It is omitted from this public package and from the source-view
image; the original Gutenberg catalogue and source downloads remain linked.
No private source downloads, research files, browser records, notes, test
screenshots or extraction tools are included in the package.

## Fonts and icons

Literata Roman and Italic are the unmodified complete variable TTF files from
Google Fonts, with their complete SIL Open Font License 1.1. The exact URLs and
hashes are recorded in the inventory. The [font licence](../site/Literata-OFL.txt)
is copied unchanged and served locally. No font is subsetted or renamed.
All 102 distinct non-whitespace source codepoints were covered by both fonts in
the source preparation; browser tests additionally exercise actual font loading.

`site/icon.svg` is an original WP monogram, and `apple-touch-icon.png`,
`icon-192.png` and `icon-512.png` are its reviewed opaque raster exports.
They contain no historical illustration or book text and use the software's MIT
licence. The icon hashes are included in the exact inventory. The reader's
ordinary UI arrows are vector symbols; literary punctuation is not replaced.

## Publication and record boundary

The public copy changes only packaging, publication/source wording, the uncertain
cover presentation and the remembered first-use source notice. Its complete
novel, chapter grouping, picture files, captions, anchors, scope gates, font files
and reading database identity are preserved. The standalone build and loopback
server enumerate their inputs and outputs; neither walks private folders into a
deployment. The catalogue mount is `/war-and-peace/` and is applied by the parent
showcase build. A new browser/origin has separate records. Text bundles retain
the novel and notices, but do not include the picture companion or fonts.
