# Sources and reuse · Pictures after dark

Eight complete French works and eight historical English translations, with a separate comparison to Baudelaire’s **Les Fenêtres**. This is a selected folio, not a complete Hugo or Bertrand collection.

The original reader code and independent commentary use the repository’s MIT license. That license does **not** relicense historical contributions, modern community transcriptions or museum reproductions. Each source keeps the terms below. The public reader is free; no institution endorses it.

## French

Victor Hugo: *Les Orientales*, collected *Poésie I*, Ollendorff, 1912, pp.709–713; *Les Voix intérieures*, *Poésie II*, Ollendorff, 1909, p.478. Aloysius Bertrand: *Gaspard de la nuit*, sixth edition, Mercure de France, 1920, pp.47–48, 53–55, 105–106, 113–114, 162–163, 200–201.

Transcriptions adapted from **French Wikisource contributors**, under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). The public source page links all eight witnesses and their contributor histories. The same license applies to any protectable modifications of those transcriptions; independent code and commentary are separate. Changes: page markers removed, prose page wraps reflowed, split stanzas reunited, headings placed in contents, typographic quotation marks and ellipses represented in Unicode. The selected page images were collated to correct witnessed transcription differences, including *nue*, *gris de perles*, *quelque affectueuses*, *héraldique*, the shorter moustache phrase and source punctuation. Le Maçon’s epigraph keeps its separate Schiller credit and italic title. Its printed defective apostrophe is retained. No silent modernization or translation reconciliation.

## English

- John L. O’Sullivan, **The Djinns**, in *Selections from the Poetical Works of Victor Hugo*, collected by Henry Llewellyn Williams, Thomas Y. Crowell & Co., undated, pp.84–87. [Selected volume](https://archive.org/details/selectionschiefl00hugouoft). The catalog’s circa 1887 date is not a printed publication date.
- Stuart Merrill, six Bertrand pieces, *Pastels in Prose*, Harper & Brothers, 1890, pp.7–10, 15–18, 21–22, 28–29. Illustrated by Henry W. McVickar; William Dean Howells’s introduction is outside this selection. [Selected volume](https://archive.org/details/pastelsinprose00merriala). Whole selected pages and their typography are retained. Print defects remain identified in reading notes.
- Andrew Lang, **The Grave and the Rose**, *Grass of Parnassus*, New Edition, Longmans, Green,& Co., 1892, p.109. [Selected volume](https://archive.org/details/grassofparnass00lang). All sixteen lines retained; no forced line correspondence to Hugo’s twelve.

These historical printed contributions have expired U.S. publication terms; ordinary life-plus 70 terms have also elapsed for the named contributors (Hugo 1885, Bertrand 1841, O’Sullivan 1895, Merrill 1915, Lang 1912, McVickar 1905). This is edition-specific evidence, not worldwide clearance. [U.S. Copyright Office duration guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ15a.pdf). Project Gutenberg was used as a comparison during preparation; these English passages follow the named print witnesses, not a republished branded Gutenberg ebook.

## Windows and images

- Charles Baudelaire, **Les Fenêtres**, complete French poem: *Petits Poèmes en prose*, Michel Lévy Frères, 1869, pp.109–110. [French Wikisource contributors and history](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Les_Fenêtres_(Le_Spleen_de_Paris)), CC BY-SA 4.0; paragraph markup converted to the reader’s comparison layout. Stuart Merrill’s complete English translation, *Pastels in Prose*, 1890, pp.174–175. Bertrand’s final paragraph is separately identified. This comparison is editorial; Baudelaire’s dedication establishes a book-level debt, not a claim that every poem derives from Bertrand.
- Victor Hugo, **Burg en ruines**, 1857. Paris Musées / Maisons de Victor Hugo, inv.46, CC0. [Museum record](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/maison-de-victor-hugo/oeuvres/burg-en-ruines). The supplied 300×216 reproduction is retained without invented detail; it is a later drawing, not an illustration of the poems.
- Jacques Callot, **The Temptation of St. Anthony**, 1635; published by Israël Henriet; third state of five. Metropolitan Museum of Art, 57.650.600, Bequest of Edwin De T. Bechtel, 1957. [Public-domain museum record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/336568); [Open Access/CC0](https://www.metmuseum.org/policies/image-resources). Resized whole image; an editorial comparison, not an established source for La Ronde.
- Twelve complete selected 1890 McVickar pages are embedded in each exact edition. The same reviewed JPEGs are separately available to the optional folio interface. Their credits and decoded dimensions remain in the edition. No other pages of the anthology are included.

The optional Baudelaire comparison and museum images belong to the application; exact literary exports carry their twelve embedded source pages. A complete site copy includes both. The original window-and-wave Home Screen mark is MIT artwork, with no historical image content.

## Exact scope

`edition-lock.json` pins the complete payloads and public runtime. The two language editions preserve all reviewed source bytes; the public package changes only build/serving, source-page access and documentation. All raw archives, personal records, private review evidence and original project history remain outside this repository. Earlier saved edition objects remain exact and exportable. Agent collation and browser checks do not establish independent human proofreading, physical-device testing or worldwide legal clearance.
