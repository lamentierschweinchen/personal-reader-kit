## The 1845 Narrative and five source figures

Frederick Douglass, *Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass, an American Slave. Written by Himself.* Boston: Anti-Slavery Office, No. 25 Cornhill, 1845. The complete source includes William Lloyd Garrison’s preface, Wendell Phillips’s letter, the appendix, embedded quotations and original source notes. Their inclusion is assessed as part of this historical 1845 publication, not solely from Douglass’s death date.

The copy-text is UNC’s 1999 Documenting the American South edition of the Murrey Atkins Library, University of North Carolina at Charlotte copy: [item](https://docsouth.unc.edu/neh/douglass/menu.html), [XML](https://docsouth.unc.edu/neh/douglass/douglass.xml). OCR: **Sarah Reuning**; image scanning: **Carlene Hempel**; encoding: **Carlene Hempel and Natalia Smith**. The prepared source retains this attribution and the underlying XML digest. The item declares [No Copyright—United States](https://rightsstatements.org/page/NoC-US/1.0/?language=en); this describes US status and is not a worldwide licence or warranty.

Five unchanged UNC JPEGs are embedded in the book: the front binding, portrait frontispiece, title page, verso and back binding. Their individual URLs, dimensions and SHA-256 values are in the inventory. The separate catalogue `portrait.jpg` is the **same 364 × 563 JPEG**, not a crop or transformation. The [frontispiece page](https://docsouth.unc.edu/neh/douglass/frontis.html) identifies the exact image as part of this source. No portrait artist or binding maker/date has been established. No separate modern illustration has been substituted.

UNC’s [digital-collections copyright policy](https://library.unc.edu/documentation/copyright-for-university-libraries-digital-collections/) and this item’s rights statement were rechecked on 23 October 2026, including the exact frontispiece route. The policy facilitates personal, scholarly and commercial reuse and says library permission is unnecessary except where the library itself owns copyright. This is the documented route for these five faithful scans, with source attribution retained; it does not convert every UNC image into an unrestricted asset. The source copy is credited to UNC Charlotte, rather than Wilson Library. No Library of Congress scan images are redistributed: that independent 1845 print witness was used privately for comparison.

The main prepared revision retains 20 sections, 97 verse lines, 52 italic spans, 140 page IDs, four original notes and all five figures. Seventeen individually witnessed transcription repairs are disclosed in the portable source context. Original note text is relocated to chapter ends with exact reference markers. Selected verse indentation was recovered from print and disclosed; some centered-line relationships and continuation quotation marks differ between the witnesses and remain disclosed. Structural, emphasis and source-map retention is not facsimile typographic equivalence. ## The 1817 schoolbook dialogue

“Dialogue between a Master and Slave,” in Caleb Bingham, *The Columbian Orator* (Boston: Printed for Caleb Bingham and Co., 1817), pp. 240–242. The contents credits **Aikin**; this package does not invent a fuller name. The complete dialogue is newly transcribed, including speaker labels and emphasis. The [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/columbianorator00inbing) belongs to the Lincoln Financial Foundation Collection; digitization was sponsored by the Institute of Museum and Library Services through an Indiana State Library LSTA Grant. Historical publication supplies the US basis. Archive access is not treated as a blanket licence to redistribute scans; no scan pixels or modern commentary are included. The source view explains the difference between Douglass’s recollection and this printed dialogue.

## The 1881 escape account

Frederick Douglass, *Life and Times of Frederick Douglass* (Hartford, Conn.: Park Publishing Co., 1881), Second Part, Chapter I, pp. 196–201. The complete chapter text, headings and argument preserve its three original prose paragraphs. The default comparison is a continuous, hash-checked excerpt with both omissions visibly marked; the complete chapter remains available.

The [UNC DocSouth transcription](https://docsouth.unc.edu/neh/douglasslife/menu.html) was prepared in 1999 by the Academic Affairs Library, funded by the National Endowment for the Humanities. OCR: **Aletha Andrew and Susan Huffman**; encoding: **Carlene Hempel and Natalia Smith**; scanning credited to **Ingrid Pohl**. The source copy came from J. Murrey Atkins Library, UNC Charlotte. No UNC images from this book are embedded. The independent [Princeton 1881 print witness, N-002898](https://digital-collections.princeton.edu/i/life-times-frederick-douglass-writt/item/0856e7d1-e7f5-4151-bdc6-30cb4a51baba) was compared throughout the six text pages. Six witnessed repairs are disclosed. Its interleaved house plate is described and omitted. UNC identifies US public-domain status; Princeton labels its scan Copyright Not Evaluated. Neither statement is treated as a worldwide licence; no Princeton scan pixels are published.

## The 1793 act

United States Congress, Act of February 12, 1793, 1 Stat. 302–305: complete **Sections 3 and 4 only**, transcribed from the [official GovInfo scan](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/statute/1/302?link-type=pdf). This historical federal statutory text is in the US public domain; see [GovInfo policies](https://www.govinfo.gov/about/policies). The scope excludes Sections 1–2, later case annotations and their call marker, marginal summaries, running heads and scan images. Both original italic enacting clauses remain. The reader distinguishes the statute’s text from subsequent history and notes that state laws and court decisions also shaped enforcement.
