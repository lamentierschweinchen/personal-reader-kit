# At Table — selected editions, artwork and reuse evidence

Prepared 18 October 2026 for coordinator review and admission at `https://newreading.app/at-table/`. This document describes only the selected candidate. Publication approval, audience and public-copy admission remain with the coordinator. No raw archives, private reading records or study prototypes are part of this public input set.

## Text witnesses and contributors

**Brillat-Savarin, The Physiology of Taste; Or, Transcendental Gastronomy, translated by Fayette Robinson.** The exact selected English digital witness is [Project Gutenberg 5434](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5434). The catalogue identifies Brillat-Savarin (1755–1826), Robinson (died 1859), and public-domain status in the USA. Credits: Steve Harris, Charles Franks and the Online Distributed Proofreading Team. Historical biographical apparatus is retained as supplied; no modern introduction or translation has been substituted. The witness contains 28 meditations and its own statement that Robinson omitted two about Paris in 1825. Complete means the complete selected English witness, not a reconstruction of the complete French work. The source does not establish a particular physical printing by facsimile comparison.

**Isabella Beeton, The Book of Household Management.** The exact selected historical English digital witness is [Project Gutenberg 10136](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10136). The catalogue identifies Beeton (1836–1865) and public-domain status in the USA. Credits: Jonathan Ingram, Sandra Brown and Project Gutenberg Distributed Proofreaders. Supplied front matter identifies original serial publication in 1859–1861 and first bound publication in 1861. The full selected digital text includes all 44 chapters, numbered material through 2751, contents, analytical index, lists of engravings/plates and textual illustration descriptions. Its HTML/archive supply no historical image resources. This is not a complete illustrated facsimile. The separately identified [1861 Wellcome/Leeds scan](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/fqt4rx3m), IIIF identifier b21527799, carries a Public Domain Mark but has not been established as the transcription witness. It is linked as a separate source; none of its images is copied into this candidate. No later expanded Beeton edition or generated reconstruction has been substituted.

**Rufus Estes, Good Things to Eat as Suggested by Rufus, Chicago: published by the author, 1911.** The selected source is [Project Gutenberg 18435, the text edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/18435), not audio record 28845. The catalogue identifies Estes (1857–1939) and public-domain status in the USA. Credits: Audrey Longhurst, Janet Blenkinship and the Online Distributed Proofreading Team; source images supplied by the Digital & Multimedia Center, Michigan State University Libraries. All foreword, autobiography, technical hints/recipes, tables, additional recipes, page-linked index and transcriber note are retained. The printed copyright notice is retained. The portrait photographer and ornament artist are unnamed in the package; no names have been invented. The package’s three historical JPEGs are embedded without alteration, rather than replaced by modern imagery.

## Notices, territorial scope and fidelity

Each selected Gutenberg catalogue expressly states public-domain status **in the USA**. Those statements do not establish worldwide clearance. The coordinator must resolve any additional host/audience jurisdiction requirements before final publication; this receipt does not claim a universal legal clearance. [Project Gutenberg’s permission guidance](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/permission.html) and [licence](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html) govern use of its name and notices separately from the historical authors’ rights. Full digital source credits, boilerplate and licence remain recoverable in each edition’s final source section and from About this work. They are excluded from ordinary chapter continuation, not deleted.

Profile `at-table/illustrated@1` preserves exact prepared text, emphasis, source references, image bytes and recoverable inset lineation. HTML normal-flow whitespace and structural separators are documented in `docs/TABLE-ADAPTER.md`. Independent checking accounts for all non-whitespace source characters in order, with the image-supplied T, and all 559 Estes internal link targets. This does not establish word-by-word proofreading or physical facsimile agreement. Historical spelling, factual errors and medical/preservation claims are not silently modernised; the reader identifies historical recipes as reading material, not current guidance.
