# Find out whether the gift works

The kit has automated evidence and an editorial brief. It does not yet have a completed newcomer or physical-device pilot. Use this bounded session before describing the experience as dependable for a general audience. Keep participant identities, book passages, notes, recordings and filled reports in ignored `.reader/`; publish only consented, non-identifying conclusions.

## Three newcomers, one complete journey each

Recruit three people who did not build the kit. Include an iPhone reader, an iPad reader and someone unfamiliar with GitHub/Vercel. One person may cover more than one category, but run three independent sessions. Use original sample material initially; a person's own book requires permission and an agreed agent/provider processing boundary. Do not start by asking them to design a companion.

Ask each person to obtain the kit from its actual public instructions and point their own agent at it. Observe where they need intervention, what they think is public, and whether they understand that browser storage can be lost. Have them open a work, choose comfortable type, use focus, select a passage across lines, write a note, leave, reload and resume. Observe native selection handles/menus, the keyboard, safe areas and landscape reading; emulated DOM selection is separate evidence.

Then ask them to visit a companion and return without losing their place, export a private bundle, and restore it in a fresh browser or second device. Check the actual complete records and exact editions, not just an import-success banner. Disconnect, cold-reload, navigate to another section, reopen the saved passage and visit the available offline companion. Confirm the origin is unavailable with a separate failed network request.

Finally, guide the person through GitHub/Vercel setup under their own account, within the existing approval/privacy rules. Observe authentication through the provider UI without collecting credentials. Verify the resulting canonical URL and direct assets, then repeat one import/note/reload/backup journey there. The presentation bundle and a successful local run do not establish this onboarding result.

Record each task as **completed unaided**, **completed after help**, **failed**, or **not attempted**, with the intervention and a concrete observation. Timing can expose friction but is not a universal speed target. Any lost record, false success, accidental disclosure, irrecoverable exit or unseen selected passage blocks a dependability claim until repaired and retested. Report all three sessions and unresolved issues; do not turn two successes and one failure into a blanket pass.

## One companion that earns its attention

Use the [Westminster Bridge brief](COMPANION-CASE-STUDY.md) or another legally reusable work whose actual structure challenges the proposed mechanism. Establish its specific edition, rights and evidence before adapting it. The existing fictional sketches are useful interaction examples; they are not proof of historical research quality.

Agree on one learning question and the reader's spoiler limit. Inspect the whole work and offer three material-led directions only if the person has not already chosen. Build the smallest interaction that tests the unresolved choice. Keep a viable outcome in which the person prefers plain reading and no companion.

Have an editor check every substantive claim against its cited evidence, separating the literary scene, proposed historical identification and present-day geography. Have a reader use the selected companion and describe, in their own words, what they learned or noticed and which exact passage it illuminated. Ask what felt generic, distracting or overconfident. Compare that answer with the intended learning; a click, source link or word count is insufficient. Revise and repeat the passage-to-context-to-reading journey. Keep the book's ambiguity intact.

## A compact private receipt

For each session, record the date, kit/application commit, device/OS/browser, origin type, source/edition identity, permissions, tasks attempted, help given, actual failures, saved-record verification, offline setup and reader feedback. Add one editorial result: intended learning, observed learning, evidence review and what was removed or revised. Name skipped checks explicitly. Do not copy private passages or participant identifiers into GitHub issues.

Until these sessions occur, release notes should say **public beta with automated browser verification**, with physical-device, newcomer deployment and editorial outcome testing pending. No synthetic fixture can stand in for those people.
