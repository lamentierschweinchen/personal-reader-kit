# Goblin Market source and rights

## Poem

Christina Rossetti, “Goblin Market,” in *Goblin Market and Other Poems* (London and Cambridge, Macmillan and Co., 1862), first edition, pp. 1–30. The title poem has 567 verse lines. The unnumbered first text page is p. 1 and scan leaf 17, with the last line on p. 30 and scan leaf 46. The original poem is in the public domain in the UK and the United States. [Scan and catalog record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Goblin_Market_and_Other_Poems_-_C_G_Rossetti_(1862,_1st_ed).djvu) · [Internet Archive witness](https://archive.org/details/rossettigoblinmarket)

The digital edition adapts the English Wikisource contributors’ [revision 13249268](https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Goblin_Market_and_Other_Poems_(1862)/Goblin_Market&oldid=13249268). The [history](https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Goblin_Market_and_Other_Poems_(1862)/Goblin_Market&action=history) identifies contributors. Five transcluded poem pages are marked validated and 25 proofread. All 30 were collated against the matching print witness, with the [University of Virginia's Literature in Context transcription](https://anthology.lib.virginia.edu/work/Rossetti/rossetti-goblin-market) consulted as a comparator. Its [institutional development mirror](https://anthologydev.lib.virginia.edu/work/Rossetti/rossetti-goblin-market) supplied the local comparison bytes. UVA’s commentary and markup are outside the adapted edition. The Wikisource adaptation is available under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

The display retains the opening small capitals, 28 verse groups, 27 first-line indents and all page joins. Line 507 spans two physical rows in the book. Its continuation is part of the same verse line. Print-page numerals sit in the margin and repeated running heads and gathering signatures are bibliographic evidence outside the poem. Typographic interword spacing and punctuation side bearings follow digital reflow. Quotation marks, apostrophes, sentence punctuation and the 13 recorded corrections follow the scan. Wording throughout follows the first edition.

## Illustrations

Both wood-engraved designs are by Dante Gabriel Rossetti. The full plate derivatives come from the two Commons PNG reproductions linked below. They are from Archive item `rossettigoblinmarket`, the same witness as the text scan. Those file pages mark the digital reproductions as public domain; this is separate from the Wikisource text adaptation. The retained PNGs are byte-for-byte originals from Commons. The WebP files show the complete plates; the cover detail crops the frontispiece.

## Release scope

The original text and both designs were published in 1862. Christina Rossetti died in 1894, Dante Gabriel Rossetti in 1882, Faulkner in 1892, and Linton in 1897. The term has expired in the U.S. and under the standard U.K. and EU terms. The contributors also died more than 100 years ago. The Mexican maximum ordinary author term consequently presents no remaining economic term. Other jurisdictions have not been individually reviewed. The adapted Wikisource text and SIL Open Font License retain their separate terms. The MIT license applies to the reader code and original accompanying prose.
