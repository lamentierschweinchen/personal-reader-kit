## Rights basis, contributors and uncertainty

The specific catalogue entries and downloaded electronic works declare public-domain status in the USA. That source declaration is the recorded US basis; it is not worldwide clearance. The modern digital transcription and its notes are retained with the complete included Project Gutenberg license. They are not assigned the historical author’s date or treated as newly licensed by this project.

Ruskin’s historical reprint notice is unsigned. Its title leaf names the National Library Association and describes a Complete Works volume; the selected digital package contains the requested Elements of Drawing. The notice says that the text is reprinted as it stood in 1859. The digitization records its own correction of a figure letter on p.58. No independent identification of the unsigned reprint contributor or print witness is claimed.

Crane’s title leaf names G. Bell & Sons, first publication 1900 and reprints through 1914. This package is not certified as a first-printing facsimile. His text and instructional drawings sit beside reproductions of earlier artworks. Their creators remain as identified by the original plate, caption, surrounding text and illustration list. A generic source description such as “figure” is retained as uncertain rather than turned into a fabricated artist credit. The transcription explains that some figures were moved nearer discussion, that page references became figure links, and that two typos were corrected. Those edition decisions remain.

Both are English-language works; no translator is identified in the selected packages. No musical-notation feature was identified in the inspected source packages. The diagrams and historical reproductions are part of the specific source packages, not modern replacement artwork.
