# Source and edition decisions

This packaged reading edition admits only the exact text, images and font files listed in `../edition-lock.json`. Its source project remains separate. No music, film, modern score, libretto or contemporary translation is reproduced.

## Text

Edgar Allan Poe, “The Fall of the House of Usher,” *Tales* (New York: Wiley & Putnam, 1845), pp. 64–82. This nineteenth-century text is public domain; no modern editorial notes are copied. The [Poe Society Text-04c transcription](https://eapoe.org/works/tales/usherd.htm) is the preparation source. The [1845 University of North Carolina copy](https://archive.org/details/tales00poeedgar) supplies the primary page images. Its leaves 74–92 correspond to the story’s printed pages 64–82. The Poe Society’s [text history](https://eapoe.org/works/info/pt025.htm) explains the 1845 choice. Research was checked 18 October 2026.

The local adapter preserves the text before the Society’s modern double-bracket corrections; it does not silently adopt those corrections. The printed `rèsonne`, `trellissed`, `sulphureous` and `Inquisitorium` remain. Page locators, running headers and the website’s apparatus are excluded. HTML indentation and prose line wraps become ordinary spaces; prose paragraph order remains. The six verse stanzas retain line breaks, numerals and alternating indentation. Epigraph first two lines are roman; its attribution is italic. The invented Mad Trist remains within the story, including its couplet. Poe’s original footnote is placed after the story and reachable from its asterisk without moving main progress.

One explicit transcription exception follows the primary printed page: block u27 restores **Œgipans**, with a capital ligature, where the Society transcription gives `œgipans`. The capital is clear on printed page 75 (UNC scan leaf 85). The adapter asserts the unique occurrence and replaces exactly one character, without shifting emphasis offsets. Both the source reviewer and implementing editor inspected this page. The original downloaded transcription is unchanged. A separate exception receipt and an independent test retain this difference rather than masking it with case normalization.

The reader is a reflowing transcription, not a photographic facsimile. Title typography, paragraph measures and source line wrapping change with the viewport. At enlarged sizes a long verse line can wrap; the original newline and indentation remain in selection and export. No modern chapter breaks are imposed.

There are 51 text blocks, 25 italic ranges and 41,780 characters including separators in this pinned edition. The exact prepared-library and source-text hashes are in `../edition-lock.json`; detailed source comparison receipts remain in the separate source project. Independent source comparison covers every prepared block with the one documented exception. A separate reviewer personally compared all 19 printed story pages (64–82), including poem, original note and Mad Trist. No further content, emphasis or meaningful verse-lineation discrepancies were found. This is a reflowing reading edition, not a diplomatic facsimile: print page geometry, running heads, line-end hyphenation and typographic punctuation spacing are not reproduced. The private facsimile receipt gives page-by-page observations.

## Review and publication boundary

A historic work’s status does not license every scan, modern contribution or recording associated with it. Institutional image policies are retained alongside the specific assets. No worldwide clearance is asserted. The public candidate is limited to this exact release inventory. New contributions or a changed audience require their own review.
