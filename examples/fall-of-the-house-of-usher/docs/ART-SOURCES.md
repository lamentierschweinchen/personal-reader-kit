# Illustration sources

Source evidence reviewed 18 October 2026. These are the two exact images retained in the packaged reader; see EDITION-INVENTORY.json for their pins.

## Aubrey Beardsley

**The Fall of the House of Usher**, 1894. Pen, brush and India ink over graphite. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, object **2021.16.16** (API ID 753521). Gift of Jacqueline Loewe Fowler, 2020.

- [Museum catalogue and public-domain image](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/753521)
- [Object API](https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/753521)
- [Met Open Access policy: CC0](https://www.metmuseum.org/hubs/open-access)

The downloaded museum original is 2314 × 3645 pixels. The candidate retains the complete sheet, paper tone and margins without alteration. The drawing dates to 1894; the related Stone and Kimball edition/portfolios appeared in 1895–96. The catalogue identifies Roderick listening after the entombment. It does not depict Madeline's later appearance.

## Harry Clarke

**Madeline at the doorway** (descriptive label), black-and-white illustration first published in the 1919 Clarke edition; reproduced here from the Duke University Libraries scan of an edition catalogued as **1923**, *Tales of Mystery and Imagination*, London: George G. Harrap & Company Limited; New York: Brentano's. Plate facing printed page **130**, Internet Archive leaf **165**. The list of plates is on printed page 7, leaf 13.

- [Exact Duke scan and plate](https://archive.org/details/talesofmysteryim01poee/page/n165/mode/1up)
- [Public Domain Review provenance and digital-copy rights report](https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/harry-clarke-s-illustrations-for-poe-s-tales-of-mystery-and-imagination-1919)
- [Maastricht University catalogue for the 1919 edition](https://digitalcollections.library.maastrichtuniversity.nl/s/topstukken/item/2422)

The 1919 date was also checked against the actual Maastricht scan: its title page prints MCMXIX, and its scan image 149 reproduces the same plate with page reference 130. Maastricht is a reference witness only; the reproduced asset comes from Duke.

The full page is 2473 × 3531 pixels. The candidate converts the original JP2 to a high-quality JPEG without cropping, retouching or changing tone; the plate's border, signature and caption remain visible. Its caption identifies the doorway scene near the ending. This is a different moment from Beardsley's drawing.

The Public Domain Review identifies the underlying illustration as public domain and reports that the source confirmed no additional digital-copy rights by email. That is a reported confirmation, **not a CC0 licence** in the Archive record. The Archive metadata contains no item-level licence. The physical title page is undated; 1923 is the library catalogue date. The catalogue's misspellings of Clarke and Harrap are not repeated here.

Duke's [Rubenstein public-domain reuse guidance](https://library.duke.edu/rubenstein/research/citations-and-permissions) says requests are not required to publish images of public-domain material. Its [general website policy](https://library.duke.edu/about/reuse-attribution) is broader and asks permission for publication unless an exception or licence applies. The scan is credited to Duke without a verified Rubenstein holding attribution; neither general policy is being represented as an item-level licence. The source-specific digital-copy evidence remains the Public Domain Review's reported confirmation.

The underlying 1919/1923 illustration is outside the ordinary US publication term and the ordinary UK life-plus-70 term (Clarke died in 1931). This is not a universal territorial warranty. The specific scan's evidence and the eventual destination/audience must remain part of publication review. Raw images, catalogue snapshots and private comparison notes remain in the separate source project's private archive; only the two reviewed JPEGs are packaged here.
