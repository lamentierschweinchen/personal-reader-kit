# A Room of One’s Own: source and rights

Original review October 17, 2026. Updated November 25 with the edition’s original-note access and short archival afterword. This record describes the selected public package.

The English literary text is Virginia Woolf’s *A Room of One’s Own*, published by Leonard and Virginia Woolf at the Hogarth Press, 52 Tavistock Square, London, in **1929**, and printed by R. & R. Clark, Limited, Edinburgh. The source is the scan-backed, validated [English Wikisource transcription of the 1929 Hogarth edition](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Room_of_One%27s_Own_(Hogarth_1929)), not the Gutenberg Canada transcription whose base is a 1931 edition. No modern introduction, translation, portrait, cover or critical edition has been imported.

[Duke’s Center for the Study of the Public Domain](https://cspd.law.duke.edu/publicdomainday/2025) specifically identifies this 1929 work as entering the **United States public domain on 1 January 2025**. That conclusion covers the literary text and quoted material as published in the 1929 volume in the United States. It is not a worldwide rights assertion. Woolf’s dates, 1882–1941, are confirmed by the [Virginia Woolf Society of Great Britain](https://virginiawoolfsociety.org.uk/resources/the-principal-works-of-virginia-woolf-1882-1941/). The [UK Intellectual Property Office’s duration guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term) gives the ordinary literary term as life plus 70 years; applying that rule to Woolf’s published writing yields expiry at the end of 2011. This review does not individually clear every historical quotation in every territory, or countries with longer terms.

The EU term for the underlying English text is ordinarily life plus seventy years. Woolf’s text is also beyond Spain’s older life-plus-eighty term. As an inference from Mexico’s pre-1994 life-plus-fifty term and the express no-revival transition in the 1993 decree, Woolf’s text had already expired there before the longer term took effect. These findings concern Woolf’s published English text. The edition distributes no modern cover, translation, or introduction. [EU copyright-term directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2006/116/oj/eng) · [Spain’s intellectual property law](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-8930#dtcuarta) · [Mexico’s 1993 decree, Fourth Transitional Article](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/ref/lce/LCE_ref01_22dic93.pdf)

The reflowed Wikisource adaptation follows [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). The edition and each chapter’s history identify Wikisource contributors. The underlying Woolf text remains public domain in the United States. The local adaptation retains the source wording, italics, small capitals, verse lineation/indentation, and all thirteen original notes. It reflows HTML indentation and printed lines, removes page-number/navigation machinery and editorial author hyperlinks, and keeps Wikisource’s chapter-local bracketed note markers. The printer’s device on the title page is **not reproduced**; its presence is documented in the source view. The app’s geometric window is original CSS artwork, with no source-image dependency.

The source-byte fingerprint identifies the snapshots; it does not prove their transcription is error-free. All 165 prepared blocks agree with an independent DOM extraction after the declared reflow.
