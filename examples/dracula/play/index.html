Jonathan Harker’s journal · Chapter III, 15 May

“That key must be in the Count’s room; I must watch should his door be unlocked, so that I may get it and escape.”

In Dracula, Harker searches the castle’s locked doors for a way out. This game invents a possible escape: its floor plan, key placement, patrolling shadows, and successful exit are fiction.

Bram Stoker, Dracula (1897). Quotation from Chapter III in the prepared Project Gutenberg edition #345. The game’s drawings and other words are original to this adaptation.

Hold the on-screen arrows, or use the arrow keys or W A S D. Two directions together move diagonally. Release to stop.

Take the key near the top of the room, then reach the gate at the bottom. You collect the key and open the gate by walking to them.

Move fully into a dark alcove to hide. In shadow confirms you are safe. Red fans show where each patrol can see. If the warning fills, you are caught; get back to cover to clear it.

Use Pause or Space to stop. Switching away or rotating a phone pauses the game too. Resume when you are ready.

This is a visual timing game. It has keyboard controls and spoken status changes, but no equivalent nonvisual play mode. Nothing is saved; a reload begins a fresh escape.
