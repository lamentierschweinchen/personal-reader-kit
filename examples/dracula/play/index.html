“That key must be in the Count’s room; I must watch should his door be unlocked, so that I may get it and escape.”

In Dracula, Harker searches the castle’s locked doors for a way out. The three castle layouts, patrols, keys and winch sequence are invented for this game. Its successful escape is not Harker’s route in the novel.

Bram Stoker, Dracula (1897). Quotation from Chapter III in the prepared Project Gutenberg edition #345. The game’s drawings and other words are original to this adaptation.

Hold the on-screen arrows, or use the arrow keys or W A S D. Two directions together move diagonally. Release to stop.

Find the objects marked on the floor. Walk close to pick them up, open a matching lock, or turn the winch. The stair leads to the next room; the south gate ends the escape.

Move fully into a dark alcove to hide. In shadow confirms you are safe. Red fans show where each patrol can see. If the warning fills, you are caught; get out of sight to clear it. Touching a patrol also gives you away.

Use Pause or Space to stop. Switching away or rotating a phone pauses the game too. Resume when you are ready.

This is a visual timing game. It has keyboard controls and spoken status changes, but no equivalent nonvisual play mode. Cleared rooms and the gentler setting are saved in this browser. Reloading starts the current room again. Retry keeps cleared rooms; Replay all rooms starts from the guest wing. Reader notes and reading progress are separate.
