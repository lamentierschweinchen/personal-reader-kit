# Castle escape

A three-room escape inspired by Harker’s exploration of the locked castle in Chapters II–III. The floor plans grow in both width and depth, while movement, characters and controls keep their scale. A 600×600 view follows Harker through the larger rooms; it stops at the outer walls. The small bearing beside the goal points toward an object outside the view. Floor plan opens a clearly labelled, paused overview of the entire room with numbered objects, Harker’s position and the current view outline.

| Room | Actual floor plan | Route and useful objects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guest wing | 600×600 | Find the brass key, unlock the dividing arch, lift the stair bolt in the west room, and reach the north stair. |
| Shuttered chapel | 900×720 | Find a crowbar in the west vestry, force the barred passage, take the winch handle from the far south gallery, and carry it to the north stair. |
| Great gatehouse | 1200×900 | Find the gate key in the far east, cross the upper gallery to the winch, fit the handle and raise the chain, then return through the great hall to the south gate. |

The later objects occupy the added floor space. The chapel has almost twice the first room’s walkable floor; the gatehouse has about three and a half times as much. Three and five patrols respectively cover their longer crossings. Each level has its own geometry and object dependencies. Room growth does not shrink the active view, characters or movement buttons; the reduced overview appears only in the paused floor-plan dialog. The outside movement controls and footer actions retain 44px targets on narrow phones and in landscape.

There is no time limit. Alcoves provide safe places to wait for a visible turn; masonry and closed doors block sight as well as movement. Brief exposure fills a warning, which drains out of sight. Gentler patrols move more slowly and allow more exposure. Pause, floor-plan visits, room interludes and the final screen freeze movement and patrol time. Continue, Retry this room, Restart this room and Replay all rooms are explicit controls. Small-screen overlays scroll when needed. Switching away, resizing or rotating clears held input and pauses active play.

Only cleared rooms and the gentler setting are saved, under the existing game-only localStorage key `newreading-dracula-castle-v2`. Its version-2 shape is unchanged, so previously cleared rooms remain cleared. Reload begins the latest uncleared room again with its carried tools; retry does not replay earlier rooms. Replay all resets this game checkpoint. Invalid stored data is preserved until an explicit fresh replay, and failed storage writes display a warning while in-memory play continues. Reader notes and progress use separate records. The separately maintained return module preserves the reader route and registers the reader’s scoped offline copy. There are no accounts, analytics or external runtime dependencies.

The successful escape, three floor plans, object placements, winch sequence and patrolling shadows are invented and identified as such in From the novel. Every SVG room mark, character, object, gate and icon is newly authored. Paper, ink and oxblood colours follow the reader. No image services, fonts or copied illustration pixels are used. Original code and drawings use the example’s MIT license.

The only quotation is a contiguous exact excerpt from the pinned edition, chap03 / chap03-b030, credited to Jonathan Harker’s journal, Chapter III, 15 May. The optional source view links to [Project Gutenberg 345](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/345). It requires no later plot knowledge. Full edition notices remain in the reader.

The game.json inventory pins every runtime file. The game regressions check increasing walkable floor, fixed view scale, camera bounds, patrol/solid separation, safe cover and item dependencies. `showcase/check-dracula-game.mjs` verifies actual keyboard completion and failure/retry in all rooms, checkpoint reloads and replay, trusted Chromium touch completion, camera travel into added floor space, full-plan pause/return, release/cancel, rotation, small-screen layouts, frozen interludes, storage failures and the exact source excerpt. Node-side route prediction chooses departure windows; browser wins are earned with real input and real animation frames. WebKit receives keyboard completion and trusted tap checks; its public automation API does not supply touch holds. Browser emulation is separate from physical-device and nonvisual acceptance. The combined catalogue release checks cover offline caching and reader return.
