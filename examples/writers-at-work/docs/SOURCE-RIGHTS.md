# Sources, rights and credits

How writers write is an original editorial collection about writing, prepared by New reading. It follows twelve selected advice lists in [Open Culture's Writing Tips index](https://www.openculture.com/2013/02/seven_tips_from_ernest_hemingway_on_how_to_write_fiction.html), supplemented by the separately identified sources in the reader. The collection contains 105 concise summaries and ten linked Atwood topic cues, six comparative essays, six two-choice writing exercises and original worked examples.

## The admitted material

The exact reviewed file is `edition/edition.json`, SHA-256 `8fb8643c04c31f9882e18c64868bb3f41219584be9fcdc987f6566c58da3a68a`. It contains twelve author records, 115 advice entries and 36 source records. Each item identifies a witness, location, context and verification limit. Thirty-seven items are marked primary or corroborated by an inspected primary passage; 78 remain secondary. Corroboration of a principle in a later interview is not proof of the earlier wording. The detailed item notes preserve that distinction.

The summaries use the editor's wording. No complete book, essay, letter, memo, transcript, author portrait, recording, source scan or downloaded source page is included. Quoted sentences in the worked examples were written for this edition; they are not passages from the authors' works. The source list is not a reconstructed conversation or a claim of publisher endorsement.

## Source distinctions

- Hemingway: the 1935 essay, later memoir, other selections and a letter remain distinct; the 1958 Paris Review interview corroborates four principles without establishing every earlier witness.
- Fitzgerald: letters, memoir recollection, an essay and a fictional narrator supply different kinds of evidence. The Rich Boy statement belongs to its narrator. Beloved Infidel is the 1958 Graham memoir.
- King: the Open Culture selection, its retailer source and authorized excerpts concern the same underlying On Writing. Four selected points have primary support; sixteen remain secondary. Learning through imitation is distinct from copying a formula.
- Bradbury: twelve points are a later editor's interpretation of a digressive lecture, not an author-issued numbered list. The complete recording was not independently collated.
- Zinsser: the university-hosted transcription supports four points; its imperfect text and the later essays are not interchangeable editions.
- Vonnegut: the eight summaries retain secondary attribution and the O'Connor qualification. The original introduction's distinctive jokes and examples are not reproduced.
- Morrison: the 1993 preview and later NEA interview have separate dates and scopes. Later testimony supports changed working habits and the character discussion, not every earlier extract.
- Poe: the 1846 composition essay supplies a retrospective account and a method to examine, not independent proof of the composition sequence. The full essay and poem are not included.
- Atwood: ten short topic cues link to the Guardian contribution; they are not a substitute for its text. The 1990 Paris Review interview is a separate work used in the commentary.
- Ogilvy: ten concise summaries describe the workplace memo's methods. The memo's distinctive insults and examples are omitted. Company attribution does not establish point-level primary verification.
- Steinbeck: six points are attributed in the Paris Review composite to the February 1962 Robert Wallsten letter. They are not answers from a 1975 interview.
- Wilder: the ten-point list is mediated by Crowe, Gotham and Open Culture; the 1996 interview supports the separate introductory example. Point seven credits Lubitsch.

The application supplies the exact linked witnesses and limits for each point. Some linked originals require a subscription. Full original books and every secondary transcription were not independently collated.

## Reuse

This package discusses methods and compares their uses through original criticism and teaching examples. The linked source works retain their own rights. The reader kit's MIT license does not license those works, and access to a web page is not permission to reprint it. No worldwide public-domain claim is made for the modern authors. A future addition of source text or media requires its own review; this publication does not authorize expanding the linked lists into fuller reproductions.
