# Poems, First Series — source and reuse

Emily Dickinson, *Poems*, edited by Mabel Loomis Todd and T. W. Higginson (Roberts Brothers, Boston, 1890). This is the [Bancroft scan](https://archive.org/details/poemssucc00dickrich) behind the [Wikisource transcription](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Poems_(Dickinson)). The retained EPUB was exported November 13, 2026. SHA-256 `3e1bd3e7a758da24f1ca1273d585df5871ae509d4f1ba70ca7e2a6c0e44db709`. The 1890 imprint is established from the scan. The impression has not been identified.

The edition contains the title and imprint, Higginson’s signed preface, the italic Prelude, and all 115 numbered poems (26, 18, 31 and 40). Contents retains their printed page references. Two scan-checked corrections in the adaptation restore word order in the preface on p. vi and a period in the source note to “Success” on p. 13. Verse lines, meaningful indents and the six-dot divider in “A Day” are retained in a reflowing layout. The digital witness received sampled scan collation. The package contains the approved reader and transcription, without scan images or modern editions.

## Reuse

The 1890 U.S. publication is in the U.S. public domain. Emily Dickinson died in 1886, Thomas Wentworth Higginson in 1911, and Mabel Loomis Todd in 1932. The historical edition falls beyond the ordinary life-plus-70 term. Mexico’s 1963 federal copyright law set thirty years from death for authored material and thirty years from first edition for posthumously published works. The relevant dates here had already run when that law was published, decades before the 2003 extension. This is an edition-specific assessment, with no universal territorial claim.
