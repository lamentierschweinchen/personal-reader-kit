# Antigone: sources and rights

The complete English prose translation is Richard Claverhouse Jebb’s *Antigone* in *The Tragedies of Sophocles* (Cambridge University Press, 1917 reprint of the 1904 collection), printed pages 125–172. It includes the persons, scene, preliminary account and closing chorus, with Jebb’s general Note of August 1904 available separately. Other plays and modern website headnotes are excluded.

## Photograph and context

The photograph is the Met’s **DT6206**, showing a mourner and ribboned grave marker on an Attic terracotta lekythos attributed to the Achilles Painter, ca. 440 BCE. Credit: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, **1989.281.72; Gift of Norbert Schimmel Trust, 1989**. The [object record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/255949) marks the image Public Domain; the [Met Open Access policy](https://www.metmuseum.org/policies/image-resources) provides its CC0 route. The unchanged JPEG is 2,978 × 3,722 pixels, SHA-256 `def6f1a9f01e2dec422022c6dc9fb7c3e3a5c21eb32fdc31fac6b378875be1f1`.

The image, credit and source notices are embedded in the exact edition and full bundles. `edition/lekythos.jpg` is the same decoded image. The contextual summary cites the Met’s account of [Greek funerary rites](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/death-burial-and-the-afterlife-in-ancient-greece). It uses this Attic object as a comparison, not an illustration of the play or proof of a particular Theban practice. Museum prose and audio are not reproduced.
