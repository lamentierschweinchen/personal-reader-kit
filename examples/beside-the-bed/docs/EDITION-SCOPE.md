# Beside the Bed — edition and recovery contract

The selected collection consists of Florence Nightingale’s complete *Notes on Nursing: What It Is, and What It Is Not* (New York: D. Appleton and Company, 1898, as identified in Gutenberg 12439) and Walt Whitman’s complete *Specimen Days*, extracted from Gutenberg 8813’s *Complete Prose Works*. The Whitman digital source does not identify an exact print imprint. This is a faithful preparation of those digital editions, not a print-facsimile claim.

Nightingale retains the title page, preface, printed contents, introduction, thirteen numbered chapters, conclusion and appendix. It preserves 195 printed sidenotes, 38 numbered footnote entries, the transcriber’s table explanation and arithmetic warning, and both transposed census tables. The first table’s source newlines have meaning despite ordinary HTML collapse. Both tables are represented as fixed source text; their figures have not been corrected. Table B uses its explicit HTML breaks as row breaks; formatting-only newlines around those breaks are removed while horizontal column spacing is retained.

Whitman retains the whole body beginning with SPECIMEN DAYS and ending with NATURE AND DEMOCRACY—MORALITY, before COLLECT. Its childhood, New York, Civil War, recovery outdoors, western and Canadian travel, and literary visits remain in source order. All 18 locally placed numbered endnotes and their continuation paragraphs remain. The source’s italic styling of every preformatted block is represented as portable emphasis ranges. Twenty-four preformatted blocks, including verse, extracts and two lists in columns, retain their breaks, indentation and stanza gaps. The containing volume’s general and detailed contents were inspected as structure; neither contents list is silently substituted for book text. Collect, November Boughs and Good-Bye My Fancy are outside this selected work. The source’s W. W. signature is retained as its own small section because it is a source heading.

The collection uses the existing `personal-reader/text@1` contract. No new literary fields are added. A source-aware HTML adapter handles ordinary HTML whitespace, emphasis, preformatted material and tables. Interactive source-note references are derived from unchanged text and section-local definitions. Exact exports contain note text in source order; no external media are needed. Long verse can wrap or preserve width without changing its characters or original line breaks. Tables always preserve columns. Preserve this reader’s source revision with private recovery bundles because its note-link enhancement and presentation choices are application behaviour.

Complete import coverage, independent browser-DOM character agreement and proofreading are separate claims. Structural and character checks are recorded privately. Representative close reading covers the four requested Nightingale chapters and Whitman’s hospital visits, the opening/endnotes, the change into nature notes, selected travel/literary pages, and high-risk verse/tables. This is not full literary proofreading against printed pages.

In this selected public copy, `npm run build` and `npm run dev` use the four pinned `edition/` inputs: `library.json`, `studies.json`, `sources.json` and `notices.json`. `edition/inventory.json` verifies each file before it enters the output. The build also retains the original-sample integrity safeguard, although those samples are not the production collection. `build({output})` accepts a project-relative output directory; the older `outputDir` alias remains supported. Absolute paths and traversal are rejected. The enclosing kit records the exact selected files and output hashes.

During the independent source project's local trial, a separate `build:shell` command and isolated shell-boundary tests protected the historical preview. That command, private release reports and the ignored `.reader/PUBLIC-INPUTS.json` handoff are not included in this public copy. Raw sources, screenshots, private bundles and reading records remain outside the public package. Full historical source credits and digital terms are included in `edition/notices.json` and available through Sources & artwork.

Recovery: Settings exports a private bundle of exact books, places and marginalia. Restore it into this matching reader, review the proposed additions, then inspect both books and an anchored note. Built-in collection editions may be restored even when the library download is unavailable; edition collisions remain fatal. No browser records are cleared during application updates. A browser that visited an earlier cached shell may need a reload after the new offline worker activates. Unsaved note drafts prevent automatic reload.

Source uncertainty: Whitman’s digital volume contains two literal {A} markers, in Soldiers and Talks and the tree list, with no matching definition found anywhere in the downloaded volume. They remain unchanged and unlinked; no missing note has been invented. This limits the source itself, rather than import coverage.

Production presentation revision: compact sidenotes retain their exact text and structural separators in the DOM and portable edition. The separators beside sidenotes receive compact visual spacing; ordinary prose and verse separators remain unchanged. Room offers its own recoverable hymn wrap choice, starting wrapped, while the ordinary reader’s source-line preference remains available.

The current one-edition and full reading bundles carry the bounded source-notice supplement described in [SOURCE-RIGHTS.md](SOURCE-RIGHTS.md). It preserves the unchanged exact books and rejects mismatched notice bytes. Restore through Settings in this compatible reader; older bundles remain recoverable with an explicit missing-notices warning.
