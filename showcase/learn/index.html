How it works

From a book to your own reader.

An AI coding app builds it with you. You bring the book and the decisions. Set it up on a computer, then read on your phone.

1

Open the right app.

First, make an empty folder called My reader. That’s where the reader’s files will live. Then open it in a coding app: an AI app that can create, edit and run files in a folder on your computer.

Which AI do you already use?

Codex, in the ChatGPT desktop app

  1. Install or update the ChatGPT desktop app, then sign in.
  2. Select Codex and open your My reader folder as a project.
  3. Start a local task in that project. Paste the instruction below.

Uses your ChatGPT account. Availability and usage limits depend on your plan.

Claude Code, in the Claude desktop app

  1. Install the Claude desktop app, then sign in.
  2. Open Code and choose Local.
  3. Select your My reader folder. Paste the instruction below.

Requires a paid Claude plan with Claude Code access.

  1. Install the Antigravity app for your computer, then sign in.
  2. Create a project using your My reader folder.
  3. Start a task in Local mode. Paste the instruction below.

Uses your Google account. Usage limits depend on your plan.

2

Paste this, and watch it work.

Add your book and idea first

When it’s ready, ask it to open the preview. Read a page, highlight a sentence, save a note and reload. your note should still be there.

3

Make it yours.

Give it your book as a plain text file, or ask it to prepare an EPUB or PDF. Keep it simple, or ask for a companion around the book’s places, ideas or history. Then read, and ask for changes in your own words:

“Make the text larger on my phone. Keep the poem’s line breaks. Show me.”

Book files you give an AI app may be processed by its provider. Share only what you’re comfortable sharing.

4

Take it with you.

Ask it to put the reader online at your own address; it can walk you through GitHub and Vercel when you need them. Only include books you have permission to share; private books can be imported in the reader after publishing. Open the site on your phone and add it to your home screen.

Open the reader from its new home-screen icon while you’re online. Check that your book and notes are there. In Settings, choose Check offline copy and wait for Available offline. Then turn off Wi-Fi and mobile data, reopen it and save a note. Keep a private reading bundle as your backup.

Publishing guide
What do I need?

A computer, a coding app, and a book you own or one in the public domain. The kit is free and open source; your AI app may have plan costs or usage limits.

What are GitHub, Node and Vercel?

GitHub keeps a versioned copy of your project. Node runs the reader while you build it. Vercel hosts the finished website. Your app can set each up when it becomes useful; check any plan’s cost first.

Will any ebook work?

Plain text works directly. EPUBs and PDFs need preparation, and some layouts take extra work. DRM-protected ebooks can’t be used. The sample lets you try everything first.

What if I get stuck?

Tell the app what you see: “I expected a preview, but I’m seeing an error. Find the cause and explain the next step.” If it only replies with code, check that it can edit and run files in your folder.

Can I make one for someone else?

Yes, with a public-domain book or one you have permission to share. Owning a copy doesn’t give permission to publish its text. About book rights.
