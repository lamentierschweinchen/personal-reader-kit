Rouen · 1550

Brazil on
the Seine

When Henry II came to Rouen in 1550, the city built Brazilian villages along the Seine. The organizers painted trees red, staged a battle and set a village on fire. The scene survives in a festival book published the following year.

Montaigne describes a later encounter in Rouen, under Charles IX. Three visitors from Brazil saw the French court and offered their own account of what they found strange.

Wood for iron

Follow the log to the water. The festival book describes brazilwood, prized for its red dye, traded for axes, billhooks and iron wedges. Small boats carry the wood to a ship on the Seine.

Montaigne imagines telling Plato about a people without trade, riches or poverty. In Rouen, trade with the French was part of the show.

Montaigne’s list · p. 255

A bed between trees

A figure rests in a hammock above the water. The festival book describes cotton beds. Montaigne compares beds hung from a roof to sailors’ hammocks. On the next page, he says examples of their beds, ropes, swords and bracelets could be seen at his house.

The beds · p. 256 The objects · p. 257

What the visitors saw

Asked what they found remarkable in France, the visitors notice armed men obeying a child and people begging at the doors of the rich. They wonder why the hungry put up with it instead of setting those houses on fire. Montaigne remembers those two answers and forgets the third.
