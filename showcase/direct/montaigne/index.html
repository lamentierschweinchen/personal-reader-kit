Rouen · 1550

Brazil on
the Seine.

On October 1, 1550, Rouen welcomed Henry II with a Brazilian scene on the bank of the Seine. The organizers painted trees red, built palisaded villages, and staged a battle. A festival book published the following year preserves this woodcut.

Montaigne later describes three visitors to Rouen during the reign of Charles IX and a conversation with one of them.

01 · Wood and boats

Wood for iron.

Figures carry wood toward the canoes. The festival book describes brazilwood, prized for its red dye, exchanged for axes, billhooks and iron wedges. Small boats then carry the wood to a ship on the Seine.

Montaigne’s list on page 255 describes a people without traffic or wealth. In Rouen, trade with the French was part of the royal entertainment.

Montaigne’s list · page 255

02 · A bed between trees

The hammock.

A figure rests in a hammock between two trees, just above the water. The festival book describes the beds as woven from cotton.

Montaigne describes cotton beds hung from roofs, like sailors’ hammocks. He says beds, ropes, swords and bracelets of this kind could be seen at his house.

Beds and objects · pages 256–257

03 · A staged battle

Fire in the distance.

In the foreground, fighters swing clubs and raise shields; beyond them, a village burns. The festival book names the sides: the Tabajaras attack the Tupinambás, who defeat them and set fire to the Tabajaras’ village.

Montaigne turns from war and prisoners to the torture of neighbors and fellow citizens under the cover of religion.

War and prisoners · pages 259–260

The historical account

Brigitte Quignard transcribes the printed festival account, including the staged exchange, and describes the related manuscript in “La fête cannibale”. Denis Bjaï examines the date of Montaigne’s Rouen encounter.
