# Books, permissions and public examples

**Use only material you are entitled to use. Owning a copy does not by itself give permission to publish it. Share a book only when the relevant text, translation, illustrations and other contributions are public domain in the applicable jurisdictions, appropriately licensed, or covered by permission.**

This is a practical project rule, not a legal clearance or a claim that every purchased ebook can be imported. Do not bypass DRM. The kit's MIT license covers its code and original samples; it grants no rights over books you bring.

## Importing and publishing are different decisions

Check permission for the intended use, including processing by an agent or external service, before supplying files. The finished reader's browser-local storage does not establish what the agent's model provider may process. See [privacy](PRIVACY.md).

A public reader application can keep imported books in the reader's browser; publishing that application does not publish those imports. Hosting the book files is a separate decision. Not charging for access or keeping the URL unshared does not itself give permission to publish a protected book.

Keep personal books, quotations, notes and reading bundles out of public repositories and hosting. A book-only export still contains the full text; adding your annotations does not make it freely distributable. Unlisted URLs and noindex do not restrict access.

For a public example, check the **specific edition and every included contribution**, not just the title or author's death date. A public-domain novel may be packaged with a protected translation, introduction, cover, photograph or illustration. Record any attribution, license, trademark or redistribution conditions. Check the territories relevant to publication and use; a catalogue's “public domain in the USA” statement is not worldwide clearance. Preserve unresolved questions instead of marking them approved.

For an adaptation trail, distinguish the original artwork or composition from the particular scan, recording, restoration, translation or later completion being offered. Record the exact contribution and rights evidence rather than assigning one date to everything. A useful original commentary can link to an official listening or viewing page without copying its media into the reader; explain that the external visit needs a connection.

For a historical scan, inspect whether the reproduction adds creative work; a recent file date or institutional footer does not answer that question. Record the territorial basis and check the provider's access/reuse terms separately, including which service and users they cover. Do not infer permission from silence. The [US Copyright Office, §909.3(A)](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap900/ch900-visual-art.pdf) and [UK IPO's digital-images guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-notice-digital-images-photographs-and-the-internet/copyright-notice-digital-images-photographs-and-the-internet) distinguish copying from creative contributions; neither grants worldwide clearance.

A single quotation or small word count is not automatic permission. For a proposed critical quotation, record the purpose, amount, attribution and applicable legal basis; quote only what the point needs and review the context. The [US Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/) explains that even a small but central portion may fail fair use and supplies no fixed safe amount. [UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/exceptions-to-copyright) separately requires fair dealing and sufficient acknowledgement for criticism, review or quotation. These are jurisdiction-specific considerations, not blanket clearance.

## A reviewable rights receipt

For each proposed public reader, record:

- Work, edition, language, author, translator, illustrator and other contributors as actually supplied.
- Source landing page, exact downloaded file URLs, access date and SHA-256 fingerprints.
- Rights evidence for each text and asset, the jurisdictions it establishes, license/permission terms and any unresolved territory or contribution.
- Required credits/notices and how they appear in the website and downloads.
- Exact prepared text and asset inventory, with the transformation/fidelity receipt and any exclusions explained.
- Intended public destination and audience, reviewed revision and reviewer/date. Keep personal contact details or private permission correspondence outside public code.

Do not treat an automated file audit as a rights decision. It can check an inventory and detect some mistakes; it cannot establish copyright status. A disclaimer cannot repair an unauthorized upload.

Review outward copies as well as the hosted reader: a standalone text quotation or image card may need credits, license links and change notices that an exact-edition bundle already retains. Follow the actual source terms for each format and bind its provenance to the verified complete edition, not just a reused title, ID or source-file hash. Preserve meaningful addition, deletion and reconstruction signs too. If an output cannot carry required notices or source meaning, refuse that format and offer an exact-edition export that retains them; do not silently simplify the quotation or attach another edition's notice.

## Public examples

Real books make valuable trials because they expose structures that convenient samples miss. Keep their source archives and preparation separate from the reusable kit. This kit's default build continues to include only its original samples. Each public example, whether public domain or separately licensed, needs its own reviewed asset manifest and explicit build inputs; do not relabel an imported work as “original” to satisfy a check or broadly permit an entire source directory. Keep a licensed modern edition's terms separate from the status of the underlying historical work and from the kit's MIT code license.

Use the actual source text, not a catalogue's generated synopsis. Preserve the chosen digital edition accurately, and do not describe that as a verified facsimile of a historical printing without comparison. Retain a source-edition e-reader link where useful, clearly distinguished from any future kit-generated export. Public-domain books do not make a reader's personal notes public.

An ancillary decorative cover with unresolved provenance may be excluded from the public package. Record that decision and verify its absence from built assets, caches and promotional copies. Preserve the source archive privately. This is not permission to drop integral illustrations, text-bearing initials, captions or apparatus, or to delete unsupported content merely to pass a validator.

## Sources checked 16–21 October 2026

The [US Copyright Office's text of 17 USC §202](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap2.html#202) distinguishes ownership of a copy from copyright ownership. This is a US rule cited for that distinction, not a worldwide legal opinion.

Its [fair-use guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/) explains that noncommercial purpose is one factor, not an automatic exemption; the amount used, nature of the work and effect on its market also matter. A polite rights reminder does not replace permission for the intended use.

[Project Gutenberg's permissions guidance](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/permission.html) explains its US jurisdiction, source credits and trademark/redistribution conditions, and cautions that its digital editions need not reproduce a single print source exactly. Read the actual ebook's notices and [license guidance](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html) for a proposed redistribution. Do not assume every item in a public-domain library has identical rights.
